I have always liked the term, “The Dog Days of Summer.” This term originated with the Greeks and Romans who believed that when two stars aligned, it caused a severe heat that could bring fever or even catastrophe. In my mind, this term is not as complicated, but rather just a term used to describe extremely hot and humid weather, which is synonymous with living in Virginia in August.

My family always had such strange superstitions during this time of the year. My grandparents would caution me not to go into the woods because they claimed that snakes are blind during dog days and because they cannot see when confronted, they strike at anything that comes near. I never once doubted that this was not true until I was a little older and then I realized that they just used this as a ploy to keep me out of the woods. They also said this was a time when dogs could go crazy from the heat, so they cautioned me to never pet a dog that was acting suspicious during dog days. As a youngster, I always wrestled with whether or not that meant it was okay to pet a dog that was acting suspicious any time other than dog days.

In the automotive business, “The Dog Days of Summer” translates to an increase in business and more sales for our Federated members. As more people head outdoors and vacations are in full swing, summer can be a tough time for vehicles. When a vehicle operates outside of its normal temperature range on any given day, a host of problems can occur. It is no coincidence that the air conditioner in your car stops working on the hottest day of the year. Water pumps, thermostats, radiator hoses, and coolant are all tied to cooling system failures. And what about batteries? On a hot day, the internal temperature of a battery can increase significantly and the added demand from air conditioning and cooling systems puts a constant drain on the battery. There is no better place for consumers to get the parts they need than from their local Federated Auto Parts stores and Federated Car Care members, not only during the dog days of Summer but during all 365 days of the year.

We have said goodbye to July which was designated as “Right to Repair Month,” but we should not say goodbye to our duty to promote this cause which is so important to our industry and our livelihood. So as August paves the way for Labor Day and summer is sprinting toward the finish line, we should continue reaching out and asking for support for “Right to Repair.” Let us make this cause our ongoing commitment every day.
