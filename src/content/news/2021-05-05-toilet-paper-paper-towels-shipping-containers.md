On April 26, 2021, the state of Virginia reported that out of the 7,521,285 doses of the COVID-19 vaccines that it had received, 6,344,228 (84 percent) of those doses had been administered. In Virginia, the vaccine is available to anyone 16 years of age and older. As a direct result of the state’s effort to get all Virginians vaccinated (along with the onset of spring weather), things are finally beginning to look brighter.

Unfortunately, for businesses who depend on products that are shipped on containers, the future is not so bright. Shipping costs have skyrocketed, and goods purchased from China are being delayed. Desperate companies wait for containers, and they must pay premium rates to get them. However, this problem is not exclusive to Asia; other parts of the world are also experiencing the same problem.

What caused this dilemma? During COVID, while many businesses were shut down, the economy bounced back faster than had originally been thought, and as a result, more containers were coming from China to the U.S. and Europe than were going in the other direction. It is estimated that there are at least three containers going out of China for every container coming in.

Why not just build new containers? Unfortunately, the pandemic has also affected the supply of steel and lumber needed to build containers. Although new containers are being ordered, they will not be ready right away, making the shortage an ongoing problem that will most likely leave another COVID-19 scar on the economy.

What does this mean for the average consumer? Back to shortages! Those luxury items that we have come to enjoy such as toilet paper, paper towels, medications, etc., will most likely once again be in high demand and short supply.

My point in all this? While we are seeing some significant improvements due to the vaccine, unfortunately, we will continue to feel the lingering effects of COVID-19 for some time to come.
