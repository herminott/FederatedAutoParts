July is one of my favorite months. Growing up in the mountains of the South, some of my fondest childhood memories are of warm days, picnics, porch parties, fresh vegetables from the garden, surprise thunderstorms, and of course July 4 celebrations.

My dad, a World War II veteran, taught me at a very early age what it means to be patriotic. He gave me a clear understanding of the many sacrifices that were made by both men and women to keep our country free.

My grandmother, who had three sons who were also World War II veterans, often spoke of ration stamps and how every American was issued a series of ration books during the war. Due to the war, there was a heavy demand of basic materials like food, shoes, metal, paper, and rubber. To meet the demand, the federal government took steps to ration crucial supplies at home in an effort to aid the military and our allies overseas. The ration books contained removable stamps which were good for certain rationed items, like sugar, meat, cooking oil, and canned goods. A person could not buy a rationed item without also giving the grocer the right ration stamp. Virtually every family in the United States was impacted.

Today, we live in a country that is so divided, and looking back on those stories, I find it hard to imagine how amazing that time must have been. Everyone making sacrifices to preserve the freedom that so many take for granted each and every day. All Americans united for the same cause. What a wonderful thing that must have been.

A few years ago, during a trip to Italy, I visited an American cemetery located in the countryside just outside of Florence. Seventy acres containing white cross grave markers for 4,399 American soldiers who did not come home from WW II. Much like the grave markers at Normandy, this cemetery is also a stark reminder of the price of freedom.

So, as we enjoy all the wonderful things that summer brings, let us never forget the high cost of freedom, and may we never fail to acknowledge and honor all those who gave us this wonderful gift.
