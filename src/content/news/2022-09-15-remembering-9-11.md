Upon the arrival of September, it is difficult not to remember the event that so profoundly transformed our country, the attacks of 9/11.

As our country reeled over the fact that we had been attacked in our homeland, the months that followed were marked by a rare spirit of public unity. Patriotism in the aftermath of 9/11 surged. In early October 2001, 79 percent of adults said they had displayed an American flag. The public set aside political differences and rallied in support of our country and our political leadership. In November 2001, seven out of every 10 adults said they were proud to stand up for America.

Fortunately, there have been no terrorist attacks on the scale of 9/11 in over two decades. We have almost all forgotten the daily color-coded security risk display by the Department of Homeland Security, red-severe, orange-high, yellow-elevated, blue-guarded, and green-low.

Unfortunately, the patriotism and the public unity that was displayed in the aftermath of the 9/11 attacks also seems to have disappeared.

In the 21 years since the terrorist attacks on the World Trade Center, the Pentagon, and the crash of Flight 93, for most of us who are old enough to remember, 9/11 has been impossible to forget. While not that long ago, we were once a country united, we now seem to be moving in the opposite direction, toward becoming a country divided. We can only hope that it will not take another catastrophic event such as 9/11 to bring us back together and unite us in this great country that we all call home.
