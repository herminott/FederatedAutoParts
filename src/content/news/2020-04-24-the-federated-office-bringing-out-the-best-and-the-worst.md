Functioning in the midst of the COVID-19 pandemic every day has been a challenge, to say the least. There are rules and regulations that must be interpreted and followed. Gloves and masks have become the new spring accessories and alcohol and bleach are the popular scents. Things that we do in our everyday lives and for the most part, have always taken for granted, are now things that we can no longer do. Going to a movie, going out to eat at a restaurant, going to church and even funeral services are just a few of the things that have been suspended indefinitely.

Times like these tend to bring out the best in people. Medical staff, doctors, nurses, and nurse practitioners have all stepped in and have put their own lives at risk to help others. Also, there are those who work in businesses that are deemed “essential” like the automotive business. Our counter people, our delivery drivers, our warehouse workers, our office staff, and all those who help keep our businesses up and running during this crucial time should all be recognized for their dedication. These people all deserve our thanks and our greatest respect.

Times like these also tend to bring out the worst in people, like the guy who buys thousands of bottles of hand sanitizer to sell at inflated prices on the internet or paper hoarders fighting each other in the aisles of the grocery store. Having an excessive inventory of overpriced hand sanitizer or riding this pandemic out on a mountain of toilet paper will not make you a hero and these are not the people who will be remembered. Instead, we will remember those who showed kindness, who went the extra distance and put the well-being of their fellow man in the forefront. People like our Federated members who have donated thousands of gloves and masks to first responders and medical facilities in their areas. These are the people we will remember.

When things are normal, we tend to operate on auto-pilot, but now that things are not, we come to appreciate the lives we live and we are forced to see what is really important. The health and welfare of our families, our co-workers and their families, and the welfare of our customers and their businesses, all become important and concerning to us. We are checking on our neighbors, picking up prescriptions and supplies for them, and we are signing our emails “stay well” and “keep healthy.” We are witnessing kind acts and compassion firsthand.

If we have learned anything from this pandemic, I hope that we have learned that we are much more than the homes we live in and much more than the job titles we hold. I hope we have learned that we are all part of something greater and that we are in this for something more than ourselves. I hope we continue to have concern for our fellow man and that we continue to show kindness and offer our help and support where it is needed, not because we have to, but because it is simply who we are.
