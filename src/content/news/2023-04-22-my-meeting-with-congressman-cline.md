When you ask someone in the automotive aftermarket to name some of the biggest challenges facing our industry today, you will most certainly get a varied sampling of answers. Supply chain disruption, which directly correlates to parts shortages, is usually first and foremost on the list. However, you might be surprised to learn that very high on the list is access to technical information, followed closely by the ability to keep up with changing technology. Both latter challenges are directly related to the “Right to Repair” legislation that we should all be working desperately to encourage our lawmakers to get passed by Congress.

A few weeks ago, I had the privilege of meeting with Congressman Ben Cline who represents Virginia’s sixth congressional district. When I mentioned “Right to Repair,” Congressman Cline immediately associated the legislation with medical equipment, which was actually a different bill introduced in the Senate in 2020. With the help of Gabrielle Hopkins, vice president of federal affairs for the Auto Care Association, I was armed with the information necessary to present my case for “Right to Repair” and the effect that this legislation has on our livelihood. The information Congressman Cline found most compelling was the economic impact that the automotive industry has on his own congressional district. It is hard not to take notice when you see the number of facilities and number of jobs and wages that are directly affected.

Congressman Cline was very gracious and I felt the time that I spent with him was extremely valuable. Congressman Cline was able to walk away with the information showing how important this bill is to his constituents and how their future depends on him to make the right decisions. I was able to walk away with the feeling that I had made a difference and had presented my case well.
