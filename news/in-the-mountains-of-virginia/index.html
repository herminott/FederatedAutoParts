April 5, 2024
leadership update

As many of you know, I grew up in the rural mountains of Virginia. The only child of two hard-working parents, we were probably poor by today’s standards, but my parents always made it seem like we were just as well off as anyone.

I am sure that I inherited a lot of my parent’s beliefs and the way that I live my life today is a residual leftover from how I grew up and what my parents taught me.

There were certain actions that would simply not be tolerated by my parents, like saying you were going to do something and then not following through and you never, ever wanted to be caught telling a lie, otherwise you would be introduced to a Forsythia switch, which was very nimble and could have a bit of a sting when applied. As I got older, I realized that it was not the fear of the switch and its sting, it was more the way I felt because I had let my parents down and that was way more hurtful to me than that Forsythia switch. Even to this day, I find it very difficult and borderline impossible to say anything that remotely resembles telling a lie.

It seems in today’s world, telling lies is perfectly acceptable and the better you are at it, the more people begin to believe it. In July of 2023, the automakers told our industry that they had resolved the Right-to-Repair controversy. What they didn’t tell you is that they just basically affirmed the same “memorandum of understanding” on Right to Repair that was introduced in 2014. They also did not tell you that by no means was this memorandum implementing a solution which would enable the automotive aftermarket to remain competitive and allow consumers to have a choice when they need to have their vehicles repaired.  What they also didn’t tell you, which is very concerning about this memorandum, is there is no penalty mentioned for non-compliance by the automakers.

In spite of how artful the automakers are at spinning the truth and as vehicles continue to be more complex to repair, unless automakers are forced to standardize and open up their data, they will always find ways to limit access to information in order to push customers towards their dealerships. I challenge everyone who reads this article to scan the Right to Repair QR Code at the bottom of this article and send a letter to your lawmakers asking them to support Right-to Repair legislation for our industry. The future of our industry depends on it!
