How did you begin your career in the aftermarket?

I started working for the Federated Auto Parts Co-Man warehouse 12 years ago and to be honest, I had no plans to make a career here. My children needed my support, so when I was offered a receiving position, I accepted with the hope of finding something else more female orientated. Once I became accustomed to the team I was working with and the work itself, I began to understand what I was doing and realized this work is not so bad after all. In fact, what I thought would be an intimidating atmosphere was quite the opposite. I had great managers, some of which I still work with, both inside the warehouse and at our corporate headquarters who were encouraging and had patience and understanding. They gave me the tools to succeed. All I had to do was listen and use those tools and the knowledge that I gained to eventually work my way up to General Manager.

What is the proudest accomplishment of your career, thus far?

Being nominated and recognized as one of the AMN Women At The Wheel is a true honor for me as is the Federated Auto Parts Co-Man warehouse management seeing my value and offering me so many opportunities throughout the years. I work with a dynamic team and I am truly grateful that I am part of that team.

What is your best advice to other women who want to excel in the automotive aftermarket?

My best advice is to not underestimate yourself and always be willing to learn new things. Take every single opportunity for training that you can get. Take those leaps of faith, so to speak. Just put yourself out there.  Ask lots and lots of questions and make sure you truly listen to the answers, even if it’s not something you agree with, because you often get information or advice that puts everything in a whole new perspective.

What are some of the ways you have seen the industry support women in the aftermarket?

When I first started at Federated, there weren’t very many women working here at all. I personally don’t think that had anything to do with the industry’s support but with women themselves thinking that anything to do with automobiles or automobile parts was a job for a man. I think more women have realized that is not true and we are seeing more women applying, giving it a try and actually enjoying the work they do in the industry.

What is one thing the industry could do better or do differently to support women in the aftermarket?

The automotive aftermarket’s support for women since I have been in this industry has been extremely supportive and rewarding. At Federated, we have women in all types of positions and departments throughout the organization who are striving to make a difference and are  recognized for their efforts. The same goes for Federated Co-Man members, vendors and carriers. I am in contact with many women from various aspects of the industry on a daily basis who have worked hard, excelled and have advanced in their careers.
