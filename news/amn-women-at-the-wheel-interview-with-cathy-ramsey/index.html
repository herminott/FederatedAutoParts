How did you begin your career in the aftermarket?

I got my start in the aftermarket when I joined Federated Auto Parts Distributors program group headquarters in 1990.  By becoming part of the Federated Auto Parts team in their early years, I have been able to learn and grow along with the group. There was a lot to learn in the beginning as I did not have any automotive industry background. I worked hard and took in as much knowledge from the wonderful team members that were, and are, the foundation of Federated Auto Parts. My roles throughout the years have included answering the phones, various types of accounting, EDI support, providing customer service support for many categories to our valuable members, to currently being in charge of accounts payable.

What is the proudest accomplishment of your career, thus far?

One is being selected to the 2023 Class of AMN Women at the Wheel. What an honor to be put into a group of talented and accomplished ladies that have been chosen this year and previous years. Another would be the wonderful relationships that I have been able to develop with our member and vendor partners over the years. We have such a great group of distributors and manufacturers that during my time at Federated Auto Parts, I have enjoyed getting to know them and helping them grow their businesses.

What is your best advice to other women who want to excel in the automotive aftermarket?

My best advice is to work hard, take advantage of every opportunity that becomes available and find a mentor that can offer advice, listen to ideas and share thoughts. I consider myself extremely lucky to have the opportunity to work with Sue Godschalk who is a great role model and one of the best mentors that anyone could work with.

What are some of the ways you have seen the industry support women in the aftermarket?

The formation of various groups that are geared specifically for women is a great way that the industry is supporting women in the aftermarket. These networking forums open the door for women to be recognized for their accomplishments, hard work and talents.

What is one thing the industry could do better or do differently to support women in the aftermarket? I think the biggest thing the industry can do is to provide reading and informative materials about the industry to high school and college level age groups. By doing this, it will help us as an industry communicate to the younger generations that the aftermarket offers diverse jobs and it is not just for the male gender. Bringing more women into the industry should help to level the “playing field.”
