Summer has officially arrived! June 20thwas not only the longest day of the year, but it also marked the beginning of summer 2024. In Virginia, summer arrived with extreme temperatures and little to no rainfall.

Although I consider myself a seasoned Virginian, I am not used to this type of weather.  Having lived in the mountains all of my life, I have been spoiled by summer days in the mid-to upper 80s and nighttime lows in the high 60s to low 70s degree range.

In the South, we rely heavily upon nature to keep us cool. Trips to the ocean, lakes and rivers help us to maintain our comfort and battle the heat. If we do experience extreme heat during the day, it is almost always alleviated with an impromptu thunderstorm, which can cool things down in what seems like in an instant.

However, this June, the mercury has continued to rise, daytime temperatures have stretched upwards, with some days reaching and passing the 100-degree mark and there have been no afternoon thunderstorms. There is little to no comfort to be found for this type of heat other than staying inside in an air-conditioned environment.

I know this sounds very much like whining (which I try not to do), so in an effort to divert my thoughts from the intense heat, I decided that I needed to know more about air conditioning. In doing some research, it seems that although Benjamin Franklin and John Hadley were the first to test the theory of air conditioning through evaporation in 1758, the person who was considered the “Father of Air Conditioning” was Willis Carrier in 1902.

Further research showed that the first car maker to offer air conditioning in a vehicle was Packard. Air conditioning could be purchased as an extra feature in the 1940-year model. It was reported that the unit was so large that it took up most of the trunk of the car.

While in the 1940s a car with air conditioning was very much considered a luxury vehicle, fast forward to today’s vehicles and all of the options that are available to the consumer. Maintaining a vehicle is no longer just about regular service, but rather about keeping up with technology. Vehicles are equipped with everything, from something as simple as blind spot monitoring, to a vehicle that is self-driven. That is why training is so important for today’s technicians. Automotive technicians need to engage in ongoing training programs to stay ahead of emerging technologies and industry trends.

I strongly encourage each Federated member to check out the Group Training Academy (https://thegrouptrainingacademy.com). This is an extremely valuable tool that you can offer to your installer customers and one that will help tie your installer customers to your business.
