A Glimpse into the Future

June 13, 2024
leadership update

I am very grateful to Larry Pavey for filling in for me by providing an article for the newsletter while I was on vacation in the British Isles.

My long-awaited trip seemed very much like a whirlwind and included stops in England, Ireland, Wales, Scotland, Shetland Islands and ended in Norway. There were so many amazing things to see. In England, I visited Westminster Abbey, Churchill’s War Room, Buckingham Palace and the Tower of London. I saw the Crown Jewels and toured the Queen’s Royal Yacht. In Dover, England, I saw the famous white cliffs. In Dublin, Ireland, I learned how to make “proper” scones firsthand from a television cooking show celebrity. In Holyhead, Wales, I visited the famous standing stones that suggest that prehistoric settlements had thrived there. In Liverpool, England, it was a given that I had to do the Beatles tour. Strawberry Fields, Penny Lane and the Beatles museum all brought back memories of growing up in the ‘60s and ‘70s. Then it was on to Belfast, Northern Ireland and the Giants Causeway. These rock formations, set against the beautiful blue water of the Atlantic Ocean, are stunning. The next stop was Scotland. Ullapool, Kirkwall, Edinburgh, Invergordon and finally the Shetland Islands, complete with Shetland ponies of course. All these places are so rich in history and I saw some of the most beautiful and amazingly picturesque scenery.

The next to the last stop on our trip was Bergen, Norway. Bergen is a very beautiful city located on Norway’s southwest coast and surrounded by mountains and fjords. One of the first sites I saw as we docked were the colorful wooden houses. I took the Flobanen Funicular up Floven Mountain. Translated, that means I took a tram straight up the side of a mountain and the view from the top of the mountain was breathtaking. I departed Bergen by train and made my way to the final destination of Oslo.

While I very much enjoyed all of the beautiful sites and the history, I could not help but notice that the majority of the vehicles in Norway are EVs. I learned from our well-informed tour guide that 82% of new car sales in Norway in 2023 were EV. In Oslo, I found that not only cars were electrified, but also ferries, buses, semi-trucks and even construction equipment. Gas pumps and parking meters have been replaced with free charging stations. It was a very futuristic sight. I learned that Norway’s grid is able to handle all of these EVs due to their abundance of hydropower as evidenced by the many wind turbines that the cruise ship passed along the way. We also learned that the Norwegian government highly incentivizes the purchase of EVs by offering free parking, the use of bus lanes, no tolls, and most importantly, no taxes on zero emission vehicles.  Unfortunately, for the small percentage of ICE vehicles in operation in Norway, gas prices are exorbitant. In addition, the owner must pay a much higher tax rate for a non-electric vehicle.  In the U.S., it seems that purchases of hybrid vehicles are growing. However, consumers are still slow to take the plunge to an EV. According to Kelley Blue Book estimates in 2023, EVs had a 7.6% share of the total U.S. vehicle market and that is a long way from the 82% that Norway boasts. It was good to get a glimpse of what the future might look like, but it will definitely take a while for our country to get there.
