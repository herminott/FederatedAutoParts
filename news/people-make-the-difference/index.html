Sue asked if I would write an article for this newsletter since she was traveling. I must admit that I did not want to agree since she is such a talented writer, and I would look very bad in her place. However, I did not want to disappoint her, so I agreed.

In looking for a topic I realized that anything was going to be sub-standard so I should go with something simple. It was a Saturday morning, and I was out picking up coffee for my wife and I, which seemed like a perfect topic. You see she is a Starbucks fan, and I am a tightwad which means that I refuse to pay Starbuck prices, but she does not like cheap coffee. Luckily the places are next to each other so problem solved, we both get what we want.

However, the big question is how can one company charge roughly four times as much for a cup of coffee as another? Is there really enough difference to substantiate that difference? So, I decided to do a comparison. The Starbucks coffee does offer more options such as oat milk creamer or a bunch of flavors like pistachio and hazelnut. They also offered a lot more “premium” drinks such as Cappuccino and Frappuccino’s so I guess they would win on selection and inventory availability.

They also have a super slick app for your phone that allows you to order ahead of time and tie it to a credit card to have it automatically charged. They also have a rewards card and told me that I only need two more purchases to get a free cup.

All of that was certainly a difference but likely only important to some and certainly would not convince me to change. However, the buying experience was very different. I used the drive through for both so that was similar. However, that is where the similarities ended. At Starbucks, the young lady was friendly, spoke slowly, and asked for my name. When I pulled up to get the coffee there was no delay as she opened the window, scanned my app and then asked me how my day was so far and reminded me that I was close to a free cup. She used my name and asked if there was anything else I needed and wished me a nice day. It was a very pleasant experience.

The inexpensive coffee place also had a drive through but the person who asked for the order talked so fast that I could barely understand. I did manage to hear “first window” and pulled up to pay where I had to wait while they took another order. They said, “medium coffee”  and ran my credit card and told me to pull up to the next window. I was handed my coffee with no conversation or offer of any additional services. The experience made me wonder how many times businesses invest in good locations, additional inventory and selection, business services and rewards, and other benefits only to lose it with their people interactions. With all the technology, have companies decided that personal interactions are no longer important? Are we no longer training people to at least be polite and friendly and treat customers like they are important? Do we realize that products and services may be so similar that the people could make the difference? Plus, training people to at least be pleasant and friendly can add value with very little cost.  I must admit that when I talk with members and suppliers it is usually our people, at Federated, that they mention. People truly do make a difference and we are blessed with some of the best!
